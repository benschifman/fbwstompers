Trio to octet

Band configurations

We play in different sized bands to fit whatever event you have planned—anywhere from three players to eight.

It starts with the core

Every configuration is built on the core trio: one rhythm instrument, one bass instrument, and one melody instrument.

Rhythm is a tenor banjo or guitar. Bass is a string bass, tuba, or baritone saxophone. Melody is a trumpet, clarinet, or soprano saxophone.

The trio is the smallest and most limited configuration—but it's also the most portable and the easiest to fit into a quiet room, a ceremony, or a cocktail hour where conversation still needs to happen.

… then add some more

The trio is fine for some events, but the band really shines with a few more instruments.

The more instruments, the more variety of sound—and the more polyphony, several instruments improvising at once, which is the defining sound of early jazz. A DJ can't do it and a smaller band can only hint at it.

Building up

What each addition brings

Core trio — 3 players
Rhythm, bass, and melody. Portable, acoustic, and quiet enough to talk over.
High harmony
A clarinet or soprano saxophone weaving above the melody line.
Low harmony
A tenor saxophone or trombone filling in underneath. This is where the polyphony really starts.
Percussion
A drum kit or washboard. The point at which a room starts dancing rather than listening.
Vocals
A singer—or two—for the numbers everybody knows the words to.
Full octet — 8 players
Everything above, for big rooms and full dance floors.
